# Scrum in a retail digital team

## In one minute
Scrum is a way for a team to deliver value in short, fixed cycles called **sprints** (usually 2 weeks). As **Product Owner (PO)** you own the **product backlog**: one ordered list of everything the team might build. You decide *what* and *in which order*; the developers decide *how* and *how much* fits in a sprint. Each sprint has a **sprint goal**, one sentence that explains why this sprint matters. Two quality gates keep work flowing: the **Definition of Ready** (DoR, when a story is clear enough to plan) and the **Definition of Done** (DoD, when work is truly finished, including tests and release).

## Why it matters in retail eCommerce
In a central digital team, many brands and business units (BUs) want things at the same time. Scrum gives you a fair, visible way to say "yes, and here is when" or "not now, because". Retail also has fixed moments (Black Friday, Christmas, campaigns), so the rhythm of sprints and release freezes matters a lot.

## The events, in plain words
| Event | What happens | Your role as PO |
|---|---|---|
| **Refinement** (ongoing) | Clarify, split and estimate upcoming stories | Bring the "why", answer questions, accept splits |
| **Sprint planning** | Agree the sprint goal and pick the work | Propose the goal and the order; the team decides how much fits |
| **Daily stand-up** | 15 min sync for developers | Listen; help remove blockers |
| **Sprint review** | Show what was built to stakeholders | Demo against the goal, collect feedback, update the backlog |
| **Retrospective** | The team improves how it works | Take part as a team member |

## Who's who in this simulator
- **You:** Product Owner, central digital team, HB Retail Group
- **BU eCommerce managers:** represent a brand (Chemist & Co, Drogisterij Prijs, Maison Parfum)
- **Tech lead:** owns technical decisions and quality
- **QA analyst:** tests and runs regression
- **Data analyst:** gives you numbers
- **Legal:** compliance (GDPR, consumer law)

## Definition of Ready (example)
- [ ] Clear user story with "so that"
- [ ] Acceptance criteria, including unhappy paths
- [ ] Estimated by the team
- [ ] Dependencies known (other teams, data, legal)
- [ ] Designs attached, if UI changes
- [ ] Small enough to finish in one sprint

## Definition of Done (example)
- [ ] Code reviewed and merged
- [ ] Acceptance criteria tested, test cases linked
- [ ] Works on mobile and desktop, all brands in scope
- [ ] Tracking events added
- [ ] Release notes updated

## Common mistakes
- Acting as the team's manager instead of owning the "what" and "why"
- Letting stakeholders put work straight into a running sprint
- A sprint goal that is just a list of tickets
- Skipping DoR and then discovering missing information mid-sprint
