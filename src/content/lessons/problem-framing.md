# From request to problem: goal, metric and guardrails

## In one minute
Stakeholders often bring **solutions** ("we need a pop-up"). Your job is to find the **problem** behind it before anything is built. Ask open questions, look at data, and write a **problem statement**: who has the problem, what it is, the evidence, and the impact. Then set a **goal** (the change you want), a **success metric** with a baseline, target and timeframe, and **guardrails**: metrics that must not get worse (margin, page speed, unsubscribes, complaints).

## Questions to ask
- What problem are you seeing? What data shows it?
- Who is affected: which brand, device, customer type?
- What happens if we do nothing?
- How will we know it worked?
- What have you tried? What do competitors do, and does it work for them?
- Are there constraints: legal (GDPR, consent), brand guidelines, deadlines?

## Problem statement template
> **[Who]** experience **[problem]** when **[situation]**. We know because **[evidence]**. This costs **[impact]**.

**Example:** *Mobile shoppers on Drogisterij Prijs leave on the basket page without buying: 38% exit there vs 22% on desktop. With an average order of €24 and 9,000 mobile baskets a week, closing half of that gap is worth about €17k per week.*

## Metrics
- **Primary metric:** one number that shows success, e.g. *mobile basket-to-order conversion from 31% to 34% within 8 weeks of launch*
- **Guardrails:** e.g. *gross margin per order doesn't fall more than 1 pt; bounce rate and page speed (LCP) don't get worse; unsubscribes stable*

## Pop-ups specifically
Exit-intent pop-ups with discount codes can: train shoppers to wait for discounts, cut margin, annoy mobile users, hurt SEO (Google penalises intrusive interstitials on mobile), and need a lawful basis for collecting emails.

## Common mistakes
- Accepting the solution without understanding the problem
- Metrics without a baseline, target or timeframe
- No guardrails, so "success" is bought with margin
- Writing "improve the experience", which is not measurable
